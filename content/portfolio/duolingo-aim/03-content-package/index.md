## The question

I wrote for one person: a beginner with a first date on Friday, in the language they're learning. The goal was content that attracts people with a real reason to learn, and the success metric was a tap on "Download Duolingo for free."

## What I did

- **Wrote the long-form piece.** "How to compliment your date so they pay for the coffee (and 3 more first-date schemes)" is a 1,041-word Duolingo blog post. Four questionable schemes from Duo carry 18 useful phrases in Spanish, Chinese, and English, each rated on an "unhinged meter."
- **Ran a headline test.** I wrote 24 headlines across six angles (curiosity, benefit, contrarian, question, number, blunt), shortlisted 5, and picked 1. The four that lost each got a new job as a carousel cover, an X post, an email subject line, and the article's argument.
- **Built 8 derivatives for 5 channels:** a 6-slide Instagram carousel, a 12-second TikTok script, 2 X posts, a LinkedIn post, and 3 email subject lines, each rebuilt for its channel instead of copy-pasted.
- **Wrote down the voice.** I studied real Duolingo posts and turned them into five tone rules ("older sister, not mom") and fifteen dealbreakers Duo never says.

## What I found

The winning headline gives Duo a selfish stake in the story and still promises something a searcher can use. One pattern held across Duolingo's real posts: every post over 1M views had no sales pitch. The product is the punchline, never the pitch.

## How I used AI

Model used: Claude Opus 5.5. To keep the voice consistent, I built a brand-voice skill (duolingo-copywriter) trained on 99 hand-tagged X posts with performance data, 6 blog posts, 5 LinkedIn posts, 14 Instagram and TikTok screenshots, and a 12-mode humor codebook. I added rules to it whenever the drafts drifted. I compiled and checked all the voice sources myself.

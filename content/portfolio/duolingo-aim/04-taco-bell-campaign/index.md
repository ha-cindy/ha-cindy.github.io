## The question

The brief was a playful, unserious push to try the Duo Habanero, launching 10/6 at Taco Bell. The challenge was getting AI image tools to make something that couldn't run for any other brand.

## What I did

- **Wrote the visual brief and an anti-brief.** The idea: Duo is never nervous on a first date. He'll take you to Taco Bell with zero shame, and he'll make you pay. The anti-brief listed what fails: anything generic or overly polished, any color outweighing Duolingo green, or a Duo with human features.
- **Built a mood board and a design system.** Color ratios (45% Leaf green), type scale, an 8px spacing grid, imagery treatments by channel, components, and 14 rules Duo never breaks. It works from a 16:9 deck up to a 24×36 poster.
- **Made three key visuals.** A found-photo shot of Duo in a drive-thru with his "Best Employee" lanyard, a "Has anyone seen Duo?" missing-coworker poster with tear-off tabs, and a bad-date scene where Duo waves goodbye and slides her the receipt. I wrote the final prompts and listed every edit.
- **Ran a drift check** to see which rules the tools ignored, and rewrote them.

## What I found

The vague rules broke first. "Green leads" let Taco Bell purple take over a whole poster, and "never draw the mascot off-model" was too vague for the model to check, so Duo came back with fingers. I replaced them with rules the tool could follow: "the product and mascot stay green," "eyes wide open, rounded wings, no fingers," and "only one face goes to camera." In a blind test, my partner recognized the work as Duolingo with no name on it.

## How I used AI

Gemini generated the campaign photos and illustrations. Claude helped with copywriting support and voice checks (using my duolingo-copywriter skill). Claude Design built the design system, templates, and the poster layout. I made the mood board in Canva. Rejected generations are in the deck's appendix.

## The question

Most retention plans spread offers across everyone, or chase whoever left most recently. I wanted a plan that spends more on the learners who have already made Duolingo a habit.

## What I did

- **Scored every learner on RFM.** Recency is days since the last real learning session. Frequency is the share of days practiced. Monetary is the plan they hold. New accounts (30 days or less) and learners silent for 12+ months are handled separately.
- **Named eight tiers after Duolingo's leagues**, from Diamond Devotees (near-daily, full-price subscribers) to Silent Silvers (free, gone 31+ days), and sized each tier's share of learners and of 12-month bookings.
- **Mapped the lifecycle** from Acquire to Onboard, Grow, Retain, and Win Back, with a goal and key number at each stage.
- **Designed triggered emails** for one high-opportunity segment, with different offers by value band and an honest price and cancel line in every email.
- **Allocated the budget** by tier, with a plan for adapting it outside the U.S.

## What I found

- Fewer than 8% of learners bring in 57% of bookings.
- Learners who practice 6 of their first 7 days are 5x as likely to still be learning later. That makes week one the onboarding goal.
- Retain holds the cash (62% of bookings), but Grow has the room: 45% of learners bring in only 20% of bookings. Part of the plan converts the 619 Ruby Regulars, who learn on 88% of days but don't pay.
- The plan puts 85% of the budget on the third of learners most likely to stay, and stops paid win-backs after 12 months of silence.

## About the data

The numbers come from a **simulated** Duolingo U.S. "digital twin" of 5,000 learners (snapshot October 10, 2026), not real Duolingo data. Prices are U.S. list prices.

## The question

Assignment 1 found where Duolingo can grow. This one asks what Duolingo should own in people's heads, and what it has to give up to own it.

## What I did

- **Wrote one positioning statement** with one target, one benefit, and one reason to believe: for Gen Z casual learners, Duolingo turns spare minutes into real progress because its lessons are free, bite-sized, and flexible.
- **Built four perceptual maps** to see which axes actually separate the brands: price × satisfaction, price × fluency outcome, commitment × method, and modality × technology.
- **Split points of parity from points of difference.** Parity: a CEFR-aligned curriculum to B2, 40+ languages, a polished app, and speaking practice. Difference: zero friction to start and keep going.
- **Picked an archetype:** Everyman, with a Jester voice.

## What I found

The obvious map (price vs. quality) only said Duolingo is cheap and shallow. **Commitment × method** was the one that showed an opening: Duolingo is the only option that needs neither a schedule nor a second person to show up. Even "casual" options like language exchange need someone else.

So the recommendation is a sacrifice. Duolingo should own the ten-minute window (on the train, between classes, the week before a trip) and stop competing on fluency. That means giving up the deadline learner, the premium-tier revenue that follows them, and the role of proof-of-proficiency brand.

## How I used AI

Researched and drafted with Claude (Sonnet 5 and Opus 5). The map placements reflect market perception and published product details, not tested learning outcomes.
